Section L — Comprehension (Short Answer) 12 pts (3 each)
3 pts per question: 2 pts = correct main idea in complete sentence; 1 pt = specific detail / example. Accept reasonable paraphrase.

1. How were women treated in ancient Egypt? Give at least two examples.

Model answer: Women in ancient Egypt were treated quite well compared to other civilisations. They could own property, inherit land, and even sue for divorce. Women who were pharaohs, like Cleopatra, held great power. However, most women were expected to run the home.

2. Which level of Egyptian society had the hardest life? Why?

Model answer: Farmers and servants at the bottom of society had the hardest life. They had to work in the fields every day, harvesting crops and doing heavy labour, without much food or rest. Slaves had no freedom at all and had to obey their masters.

3. How did Arliss get hurt? Explain in detail.

Model answer: Arliss was hurt by the fins of a large catfish that Old Yeller caught. When Arliss jumped on the catfish, the sharp spike on its fin cut into him, causing him to bleed. He was in a lot of pain from the fin stab (finned him).

4. What story did Arliss tell Mama? Do you think she believed him?

Model answer: Arliss told Mama that Old Yeller had caught the big catfish and that he had helped. He claimed he had done most of the catching. Most likely Mama did NOT believe him because Arliss was known to be a liar — she was used to his wild stories and usually punished him for them anyway.

For each oral question: 2–3 pts. Exam Must-Know questions are marked with a red badge.
EXAM MUST-KNOW Oral Q1 — Hieroglyphics
Q: What were hieroglyphics? How were they used?
"Hieroglyphics were a system of writing used by ancient Egyptians. They used symbols and pictures to write words and sounds. They wrote on papyrus, tomb walls, and artifacts."
Key words: symbols / pictures / writing / ancient Egyptians / papyrus

EXAM MUST-KNOW Oral Q2 — Osiris weighing the heart
Q: Why would Osiris weigh someone's heart? What did this mean?
"Osiris was the god of the dead. Egyptians believed that after you died, Osiris would weigh your heart against a feather. If your heart was light (you lived a good life), your soul could continue. If your heart was heavy (you did bad things), you would not go to the afterlife."
Key words: Osiris / heart / feather / afterlife / good life / judgment
3 = complete + on-topic | 2 = partial | 1 = attempted
EXAM MUST-KNOW Oral Q3 — When were mummies made?
Q: When would people in ancient Egypt be made into mummies?
"People were mummified after they died. The Egyptians believed that the body needed to be preserved so the soul could return to it in the afterlife. Pharaohs and wealthy people were most commonly mummified."
Key words: after death / soul / preserved / afterlife / pharaohs
3 = complete | 2 = partial | 1 = attempted
EXAM MUST-KNOW Oral Q4 — Where would bodies and souls go?
Q: Where would the dead bodies and souls go after death?
"The dead bodies of important people were placed in tombs or pyramids. Their souls were believed to travel to the afterlife — a world similar to Egypt. They needed food, furniture, and treasures buried with them for the journey."
Key words: tombs / pyramids / afterlife / soul / journey / treasures
3 = complete | 2 = partial | 1 = attempted
EXAM MUST-KNOW Oral Q5 — Who was Anubis?
Q: Who was Anubis? What was his role?
"Anubis was the ancient Egyptian god of the dead. He had a human body and the head of a jackal. His job was to guide the souls of the dead to the afterlife and to watch over the mummification process."
Key words: god of dead / jackal head / guide souls / mummification
3 = complete | 2 = partial | 1 = attempted
Oral Q6 — What did Arliss love doing?
Q: What did Arliss love doing? Give some examples.
"Arliss loved to capture every living thing that he could find. He caught frogs, lizards, snakes, possums, birds, and even a copperhead snake. He kept them in his pockets or carried them around."
Key words: capture / living things / pockets / frogs / snakes / copperhead
3 = complete + 2 examples | 2 = main idea only | 1 = attempted
Oral Q7 — What happened with the copperhead?
Q: What happened with the copperhead snake?
"Arliss had put a copperhead snake in his pocket. When Mama reached into his pocket, the snake almost bit her. After that, Mama made Travis teach Arliss which creatures were safe to catch. But Arliss still kept catching creatures anyway."
Key words: copperhead / almost bit Mama / pocket / Travis / teach / still captured
3 = complete sequence | 2 = partial | 1 = attempted
Oral Q8 — Why didn't Egyptians live past their thirties?
Q: Why do you think most Egyptians didn't live past their thirties?
"Most Egyptians didn't live past their thirties because life was very hard. They didn't have modern medicine, so even small injuries or illnesses could be deadly. Children especially died young. Hard physical labour, poor diet, and dangerous animals like crocodiles and hippos also shortened lives."
Key words: no medicine / illness / children / hard labour / dangerous animals
3 = two or more reasons + complete sentence | 2 = one reason | 1 = attempted
